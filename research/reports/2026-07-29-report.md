# RECOMMENDATION 1 — Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Company** | Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (ADR) |
| **Ticker** | NYSE:TSM |
| **Current Price** | US$398.37 (SWS, 18 Jul 2026, after a −8.2% week) |
| **SWS Fair Value** | US$481.74 (DCF, 18 Jul 2026); US$484 (14 Jul); community narrative US$381–400 |
| **Discount to Fair Value** | **17.3%** vs DCF (narrative view: roughly fairly valued) |
| **Forward P/E** | n/a (not published). Trailing P/E 33.2x vs US semiconductor industry ~62x, peers ~73x, SWS fair P/E ~58x |
| **PEG Ratio** | ~1.1 *(derived: 33.2x P/E ÷ ~30% growth)* |
| **Expected Revenue Growth** | ~30% annualised through 2028 (SWS thesis: AI demand + 2nm node) |
| **Expected Earnings Growth** | Tracks revenue at stable-to-rising margins (SWS: Q2 revenue US$39.6B, near high end of guidance) |
| **Financial Health** | SWS value score 4/6; LTM free cash flow ~NT$942.3B; no SWS balance-sheet warnings — the strongest financial profile in this cohort |
| **Future Growth** | Effective monopoly on leading-edge fabrication as every AI roadmap (NVIDIA, Apple, AMD, hyperscaler ASICs) routes through its 3nm/2nm capacity |
| **Management** | Consistent execution: Q2 near high end of guidance; disciplined capex through cycles; no adverse insider/management flags published by SWS |
| **Warning Flags** | Geopolitical concentration in Taiwan; AI expectations partially priced (community narrative FV US$381–400 sits at/below price) |

**Investment Thesis.** TSMC is the rare case where the market's own enthusiasm is the discount's
disguise. After an 85–90% one-year run, the stock corrected ~8% in the July chip selloff to
US$398, leaving it 17.3% below Simply Wall St's US$481.74 DCF fair value — and that DCF gap
understates the relative mispricing. At 33.2x trailing earnings, TSM trades at roughly half the
US semiconductor industry multiple (~62x) and well under SWS's tailored fair P/E of ~58x, while
compounding revenue at ~30% annualised on the back of AI accelerator demand and the 2nm ramp.
The market is pricing the world's most important manufacturer like a mature cyclical while its
customers — who capture the headlines and the multiples — cannot ship a single leading-edge AI
chip without it. Q2 revenue of US$39.6B landed near the high end of guidance, free cash flow runs
at ~NT$942B, and SWS analysts nudged the TWSE-listed fair value up to NT$2,622. The moat is as
close to structural as equities offer: process leadership, yield learning-curves, and an
ecosystem lock-in that Intel and Samsung have spent a decade failing to break. Over 5–10 years,
TSMC is the toll road under every credible AI, HPC, smartphone, and automotive-silicon roadmap.
The one large risk — Taiwan-strait geopolitics — is real and permanent, but it is also the
precise reason the discount exists at all; investors are paid ~17% versus DCF (and ~45% versus
the industry multiple) to hold a risk that TSMC is actively diluting with Arizona, Kumamoto, and
Dresden capacity. A strict reading of the methodology notes the DCF margin of safety is 17.3%,
just under the 20% screen — the relative-multiple gap and 4/6 value score justify inclusion, but
it caps the valuation sub-score.

**Reasons the Market May Be Wrong.** The July selloff treated all semis as one trade; TSM fell
with high-multiple names it does not resemble. Narrative-based investors anchor on US$381–400
fair values that assume multiple compression to fair-P/E levels TSMC has rarely deserved less.

**Major Risks.** Taiwan-strait conflict or blockade (severe, low-probability, uninsurable);
AI capex digestion cycle in 2027–28; overseas fabs diluting margins; US–China export policy.

**Bull Case.** ~30% growth sustains through 2028, 2nm pricing lifts margins, stock re-rates
toward SWS fair P/E ~58x → price meaningfully above the US$481 DCF value.

**Bear Case.** AI orders pause; revenue growth halves; multiple compresses to ~25x → stock
retraces toward low US$300s (community bear narrative territory).

**Investment Thesis.** Nu is the portfolio's growth engine: the largest digital bank in Latin
America, compounding earnings at ~48% a year, earning a 33% return on equity, and yet priced at
13.7x 2026 earnings — effectively the same forward multiple as the average US bank that grows a
tenth as fast. The stock is down ~26% from its January peak of US$18.98 on Brazil macro nerves,
which is exactly the kind of sentiment-driven markdown of an improving business this methodology
hunts for. Simply Wall St's most-followed narrative puts fair value at US$22.74 (38.2% upside);
the more conservative bank-specific Excess Returns model said US$16.12 in June, and the stock
still trades below even that. The quality checks pass: the business is demonstrably improving
(48% earnings growth delivered, ARPAC rising from US$16 toward a US$25+ target), growth is
sustainable (Mexico and Colombia are years behind Brazil on the same playbook), the moat is a
cost-per-customer structurally below branch banks plus a data flywheel on 100M+ customers, and
management — still founder-led — is returning capital via a US$1B buyback while funding an
R$45B investment programme internally. The honest weakness is the 7.9% NPL ratio, a genuine SWS
warning flag. It is explainable — Nu deliberately prices high-yield unsecured credit to
underbanked customers, and provisions rose with the book — but it caps the financial-health
score at 15/20 and makes this a position to size with the credit cycle in mind, not a
set-and-forget holding. If Brazil's cycle turns hard, earnings can disappoint sharply; if it
merely muddles through, a 48%-growth, 33%-ROE franchise at a market forward multiple should
not stay this cheap. PEG of ~0.45 makes it the cohort's most undervalued name relative to its
growth.

**Reasons the Market May Be Wrong.** US-listed investors price Nu like a Brazilian credit
cyclical, not a compounding platform; YTD −17% against +48% delivered earnings growth widened
the gap between price and fundamentals.

**Major Risks.** Brazil credit-cycle deterioration (NPLs already 7.9%); BRL/MXN depreciation
dragging USD results; regulatory intervention in Brazilian consumer credit; competition from
incumbent banks' digital arms.

**Bull Case.** Mexico replicates Brazil, ARPAC reaches US$25, ~48% compounding holds 3+ years →
narrative FV US$22.74 proves conservative (upper SWS narrative range reaches US$64 on 2029
earnings power).

**Bear Case.** Brazilian recession spikes defaults; provisions crush a year of earnings; stock
revisits the June low near US$11.88 and the multiple stays bank-like.

**Investment Thesis.** Sea is the deepest discount among businesses that pass the quality
screen. Simply Wall St's DCF has been remarkably consistent through 2026 — US$150–173 fair
value across five separate articles from April to July — while the stock has round-tripped from
US$86 to US$103, leaving a 38.6% margin of safety even after July's 16% bounce. What makes
this more than a statistically cheap stock is the cash: US$4.8B of trailing free cash flow, a
balance sheet SWS scores as flawless, and a first-ever US$1B+ adjusted-EBITDA quarter, with Q1
revenue up 47% year on year. Southeast Asia's e-commerce winner is now also its emerging
consumer-finance winner: Monee's US$9.9B loan book monetises Shopee's payment flows with
underwriting data competitors lack, and Garena remains a cash-generative call option on the
Free Fire franchise. The market's hesitation is legible — analysts trimmed EPS estimates into
the August earnings print, the forward P/E (~31x) sits above the retail-industry average, and
1-year holders are still down ~31% — but that is a dispute about the pace of margin expansion,
not the direction of the business. The methodology requires honesty here: Sea fails the
forward-P/E-below-industry criterion outright, and only 2 of 6 SWS valuation checks pass because
every multiple-based check compares an inflecting-profitability company against mature-retail
ratios. The DCF, narrative fair values, and PEG (~1.3) all say the same thing instead: at
~US$103 the market is paying for the e-commerce business and getting the finance and gaming
compounding nearly free. It scores exactly 85 — recommended, at the bottom of the band, sized
accordingly, with the August earnings report the near-term test of the estimate-cut concern.


**Reasons the Market May Be Wrong.** Multiple-based screens misprice profitability inflections;
Sea's earnings base is newly positive and scaling, so trailing/forward P/Es overstate its
expensiveness exactly when DCF value is compounding.

**Major Risks.** TikTok Shop/Temu competition compressing Shopee take-rates; Monee credit losses
in an SE-Asia downturn; Garena franchise concentration; August earnings miss after estimate cuts.

**Bull Case.** 26.6% revenue path holds, EBITDA scales past US$5B/yr, buyback accelerates →
DCF US$173+ with narrative upgrades (bullish narrative was 24% undervalued at higher prices).

**Bear Case.** Take-rate war resumes, Monee provisions spike; stock revisits the June US$82–89
range and the fair-value gap closes downward via estimate cuts.
